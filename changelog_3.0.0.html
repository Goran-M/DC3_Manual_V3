Cabin Combustion Heater System NEW

Full simulation of the Stewart-Warner (Janitrol) S-100 combustion heater per T.O. 1C-47-2 Section IV. Burns aviation gasoline from the right main tank in a sealed combustion chamber, with a heat exchanger transferring heat to cabin air via squirrel-cage blower.

State Machine (9 States)

  • OFF: Master switch off, no power, or prerequisites not met
  • PRE_IGNITION: Fuel pump priming, solenoids opening (~1 sec)
  • IGNITION: Spark plug firing, waiting for combustion (cold weather extends)
  • IGNITION_FAILED: Failed to light, 3-second pause before automatic retry
  • COMBUSTION: Fuel burning, discharge duct temp rising toward 130°C
  • CYCLING_OFF: Fenwal thermostat opened at 130°C, fuel cut, coasting down
  • CYCLING_ON: Thermostat closed at 120°C, re-igniting (faster than initial start)
  • OVERHEAT: Discharge duct temp hit 176°C (350°F), drop-out switch activating
  • DROPOUT_LATCHED: Drop-out fuse blown, heater locked out until maintenance reset

Thermal Model

  • Combustion chamber temp: Internal, ramps toward ~450°C during combustion, decays toward ambient when off
  • Discharge duct temp: Cockpit gauge reading, lags behind chamber due to heat exchanger thermal mass. Fenwal switches sense this temperature. Normal cycling range 120-130°C
  • Outlet temp: Cabin delivery temperature, further reduced by heat exchanger efficiency (max ~80°C)
  • Blower speed affects heat transfer rate through the exchanger
  • Cold ambient temperatures slow warm-up (up to 3x slower below -30°C)

Cold Weather Ignition

  • Probability-based ignition model affected by outside air temperature
  • Above 0°C: guaranteed ignition on first attempt
  • 0°C to -20°C: success rate decreases linearly (100% to 30% per attempt)
  • Below -20°C: 30% per attempt with extended ignition time (6 seconds)
  • Cumulative probability across retries (up to 5 attempts)

Fuel Consumption

  • Consumes avgas from right main tank (0.8 GPH low blower, 1.5 GPH high blower)
  • Fuel starvation: heater extinguishes if tank runs empty
  • Fuel solenoid stuck open failure: leaks ~0.15 GPH even when heater is off

Ground Blower Interlock

  • On ground, ram air unavailable for combustion
  • Ground blower requires HEATER_GROUND_CTRL CB + both engines above 1200 RPM (or GPU connected)
  • In flight, ram air supplies combustion airflow automatically

Overheat Protection

  • Fenwal drop-out switch: One-shot thermal fuse at 176°C (350°F)
  • Permanently disables heater until maintenance reset (ground only)
  • Heater-inoperative warning light illuminates
  • Maintenance reset required, on the ground only

Failure Modes (8 Types)

  • Thermostat stuck closed: No cycle-off, temperature rises past 130°C toward dropout
  • Thermostat stuck open: Won't re-light after cycling off, temperature decays to ambient
  • Ignition failure: Spark plug dead, heater cannot light
  • Fuel pump failure: No fuel pressure, flame extinguishes or can't start
  • Fuel solenoid stuck open: Fuel leak when heater off (fire hazard)
  • CO contamination: Heat exchanger crack leaks carbon monoxide into cabin air
  • Compartment fire: Heater compartment fire (cascading failure or solenoid leak)
  • Drop-out switch failure: No overheat protection (safety device bypass)
  • Cascading: Thermostat stuck closed + dropout switch failure leads to runaway overheat and compartment fire
  • All failures use MTBF-based random triggering and scheduled failure modes

Carbon Monoxide

  • CO builds up gradually when heat exchanger crack failure is active during combustion
  • Cabin CO concentration is published in parts per million
  • Dissipates when heater off or vents open

Fire Extinguisher Integration

  • Heater compartment fire triggers cabin fire warning light
  • Fire extinguisher selector valve has HEATER COMPT position
  • CO₂ discharge to heater compartment suppresses fire
  • Heater remains locked out (DROPOUT_LATCHED) after fire suppression

Electrical Load

  • Blower motor: 3A (low) / 6A (high) on HEATER_CONTROL CB
  • Ignition unit: 2A during ignition and cycling-on states
  • Fuel pump: 1.5A (Adel 21990)
  • Fuel solenoids: 0.5A when open
  • Ground blower motor: 45A on HEATER_GROUND_CTRL CB

Hydraulic Cowl Flap System REWRITE

Complete rewrite based on Quebecair DC-3 General Arrangement Part 3, paragraphs 40-44. Replaces the previous electric-motor model with the authentic hydraulic four-way control valve described in the Quebecair AOM. Cross-checked against T.O. 1C-47-2 Para 3-22 (Hydraulic System Applications list, which explicitly names cowl flaps as a hydraulic consumer).

Architecture

  • Two completely independent hydraulic circuits, one per engine, fed from the brake pressure line with returns to the brake return line
  • Four-way ball-type control valves on the right side wall adjacent to the First Officer's seat
  • Double-acting chrome-moly strut mounted on the lower segment of the anti-drag ring, piston rod connected to the cowl flap control tie rods
  • Integral pressure relief valve per side, set to 1150 ±50 PSI, protecting against thermal expansion and excessive aerodynamic loads (Quebecair AOM Para 42)
  • No electrical involvement at all: the previous COWL_FLAPS_L and COWL_FLAPS_R circuit breakers have been removed from the secondary bus panel, along with the CowlFlapMotor electrical stub

Four-Position Detented Selector Valve

  • OFF - All valve ports blocked. Fluid is trapped in both ends of the strut, hydraulically locking the piston. The flap cannot move regardless of hydraulic pressure, engine state, or aerodynamic load.
  • CLOSE - Fluid drives the strut toward closed. Pressure-gated and speed-scaled.
  • TRAIL - Both ends of the strut connected together. Hydraulic resistance is removed and the flap floats to aerodynamic equilibrium, typically 10-30% open depending on indicated airspeed. On the ground (below 5 kts) with no airflow, the flap holds its current position.
  • OPEN - Fluid drives the strut toward full open. Pressure-gated and speed-scaled.

Hydraulic Pressure Dependency

  • Minimum pressure for flap movement: 400 PSI (estimated; manual is silent on this threshold but it sits between accumulator pre-charge 250 PSI and regulator cut-in 650 PSI)
  • Rated speed at regulator cut-out (875 PSI): approximately 8 seconds full travel, linearly scaled for partial pressure
  • With both engines running and the main system at normal operating pressure, flaps respond immediately
  • With engines stopped and the system depleted, the pilot must stroke the hand pump to pre-charge the accumulator before the flaps will move. Each full cycle draws pressure from the accumulator. This matches the procedure shown in period instruction films
  • Pressure draw while driving: ~3 PSI/sec from the main system (estimated, matches wing flap consumer pattern)

Actual cowl flap position, the detented valve selector position and the rotary knob animation are published per engine. Both the selector state and the flap position persist across flights.

Operating Notes

  • OFF is a true hydraulic lock, not a "motor idle" position. A flap set at half-open with the selector in OFF stays at half-open even if hydraulic pressure is lost afterward
  • TRAIL is aerodynamic float, not a fixed intermediate position. Previous electric motor code treated TRAIL as a 35% target; the real valve simply connects both strut chambers and lets the flap find its own balance
  • The engine cover interlock continues to block the TRAIL and OPEN positions while an engine cover is installed, allowing only OFF and CLOSE
  • Valve selector state and actual flap position both persist in LES.ini across flights

Known Limitations

  • The Quebecair manual is silent on minimum actuation pressure, nominal travel time at rated pressure, stroke volume, and exact TRAIL aerodynamic equilibrium curve. The values used (400 PSI min, 8 sec at rated, linear speed scaling, IAS-driven TRAIL target) are engineering estimates documented inline in the source code and may be refined if primary-source data becomes available

CUSTOM ICING PHYSICS ENGINE

Messinger Heat Balance Model NEW

Complete replacement of X-Plane's built-in icing with a physics-driven custom model based on the Messinger heat balance equation (1953). The engine computes ice accretion from first principles using thermodynamic energy balance at each surface.

  • 12 independent ice zones tracked: wing leading edges (L/R), horizontal stabilizer, vertical stabilizer, propeller blades (L/R), pitot tubes (L/R), windshield, antenna, and cowl inlets (L/R)
  • Per-zone energy balance: kinetic and aerodynamic heating vs convective, evaporative, and sensible cooling
  • Freezing fraction determines ice type: rime (opaque, rough, sheds well), glaze (clear, smooth, adhesive, hard to shed), or mixed
  • Collection efficiency computed from Stokes number and body geometry; small bodies (pitot) collect ice faster than large surfaces (wing)
  • Convective heat transfer via Reynolds/Nusselt with Sutherland viscosity correction and laminar/turbulent flow regimes
  • Churchill-Bernstein correlation for cylindrical bodies (pitot, antenna)
  • Surface skin temperature model with per-zone thermal time constants
  • Natural sublimation when not in active icing conditions

Weather-Driven LWC/MVD Estimation NEW

Since X-Plane does not expose Liquid Water Content or Mean Volume Diameter directly, the engine estimates them from available weather data:

  • Cloud layer analysis from XPLMGetWeatherAtLocation() determines in-cloud conditions
  • Cloud type classification (stratus, cumulus, cumulonimbus) based on depth, coverage, and temperature
  • LWC estimated by cloud type: 0.15-0.25 g/m³ (stratus), 0.3-0.7 g/m³ (cumulus), 0.5-1.5 g/m³ (Cb)
  • MVD estimated by cloud type: 15-20 μm (stratiform), 18-25 μm (cumulus)
  • Precipitation augments LWC; freezing rain/drizzle gives large MVD (100-500 μm, SLD conditions)
  • Icing severity classification: none, trace, light, moderate, severe

De-Ice Boot Integration NEW

The icing engine integrates with the existing pneumatic de-ice boot system for realistic ice shedding:

  • Boot shedding effectiveness depends on ice thickness: boots are ineffective below 3 mm (ice too thin to crack), optimal at 6-25 mm
  • Ice type matters: rime ice sheds well (1.0x effectiveness), mixed ice sheds moderately (0.75x), glaze ice sheds poorly (0.45x due to adhesion)
  • Inflation pressure must exceed 6 PSI for effective shedding (linear ramp from 4-8 PSI)
  • Intercycle residual ice: boots never shed 100% of ice; residual ice accumulates between cycles
  • Very thick ice (>25 mm) is harder to shed, with decreasing effectiveness
  • Critical realism: pilot must wait for sufficient buildup before cycling boots, matching real DC-3 procedures

Propeller Alcohol Anti-Ice Integration NEW

  • Queries existing prop anti-ice alcohol pump state
  • Effective ice prevention above -15°C OAT; partial protection below
  • RPM affects alcohol distribution (centrifugal force at blade roots)
  • Empty alcohol tank provides no protection

Pitot Heat Model NEW

  • 162W per pitot tube heating element (5.77A at 28V per AN 01-40NK-2)
  • Sufficient to prevent icing under normal conditions
  • Overwhelmed in severe freezing rain (SLD)
  • Requires electrical power and functioning circuit breakers

Windshield and Ground Frost NEW

  • Windshield collects ice at reduced rate (flat surface, oblique angle)
  • Wipers can partially clear thin frost (30% effectiveness) and thin rime (10%), but not glaze ice
  • Ground frost forms on parked aircraft when OAT is below 0°C and surface temperature is below dewpoint
  • Frost persists across sessions via state persistence
  • X-Plane handles windshield frost/ice visual rendering

Aerodynamic Penalties NEW

  • Lift coefficient (CL) reduction: ~0.8% per mm of wing leading edge ice (glaze 1.2x penalty, rime 0.6x)
  • Drag coefficient (CD) increase: ~4 drag counts per mm of wing ice, glaze 1.5x multiplier
  • Stall angle of attack reduction: ~0.2 degrees per mm of wing ice (capped at 8 degrees total)
  • Total ice mass tracked across all zones (25-75 kg at moderate accumulation)
  • Writes X-Plane's own airframe, pitot, propeller, inlet and window ice states each frame

Icing Failure Modes (6 Types) NEW

  • Pitot heat element failure (L/R): Heating element burned out, pitot ices up despite switch being on (MTBF: 5000 hrs)
  • De-ice boot distributor motor failure: No boot cycling (MTBF: 4000 hrs)

  • All failures available in the failure scheduling UI under "Ice Protection"

FAILURE SYSTEM EXPANSION

Full Failure Catalogue ENHANCED

The 2.0 failure module was two rules and a global MTBF: engine failure above 4000 prop RPM sustained for five seconds, and an engine fire if oil pressure stayed above 115 PSI for thirty seconds. It also accumulated engine "fatigue points" from low oil temperature operation. The C++ system replaces this with a catalogue of 71 discrete failure types across eleven groups, each with its own trigger logic, symptoms and repair path.

  • Engine (14): left, right or both magnetos per engine, low oil pressure, oil leak, partial power loss, complete failure
  • Electrical (8): generator failed open, generator failed shorted and voltage regulator failure on each side, battery internal failure, inverter failure
  • Propeller (2): feather button auto-popout switch stuck, per engine
  • Circuit breakers (9): fuel boost pumps, pitot heat, autopilot, inverter, gyro horizon, directional gyro, turn and bank. These are repairable in flight by resetting the breaker
  • Thermal relief valves (5): main hydraulic, left and right brake, gear, cowl
  • Cabin heater (8): as described in the heater section above
  • Ice protection (6): as described in the icing section above
  • Landing gear (7): actuator seal leak per side, worn downlock per side, jammed uplock per side, and bungee shock cords past their two-year service life
  • Flaps (4): actuator seal leak per side, relief valve stuck open, asymmetric rigging
  • Vacuum (3): left and right engine-driven pump with severity encoding, plus a check valve stuck open so a dead pump's side becomes a vacuum leak
  • Compass and gyro instruments (5): KG 102A directional gyro dead or tumbled, KMT 112 / KA 51B slaving lost so the gyro free-drifts, KI 525A HSI indicator itself dead, copilot vacuum attitude gyro seized, copilot electric turn-and-bank rate gyro failed

Failure Triggering and Management NEW

  • Four independent trigger paths: MTBF-based random failures tied to flight hours, wear-based degradation tied to component health from the maintenance record, abuse-induced failures from exceeding CHT, oil temperature, manifold pressure or RPM limits, and manual triggers for training
  • Scheduled failures: arm a specific failure to occur after a set time, at an altitude, or at a speed, for practising a known emergency
  • Severity control: failures are not all binary. Vacuum pumps, oil pressure and partial power failures carry a severity level so you get a degrading system rather than a switch
  • Cascading: one failure can trigger related failures, for example a heater thermostat stuck closed combined with a failed drop-out switch leading to a compartment fire
  • Session or persistent: a popup asks whether triggered failures should clear at the end of the session or follow the airframe. Active failures, configuration, schedules and abuse accumulators all persist to LES.ini
  • Difficulty scaling: MTBF probability and abuse thresholds are multiplied by the active difficulty preset
  • All of this is exposed in a dedicated FAILURES tab rather than the single MTBF slider of 2.0

LANDING GEAR AND FLAPS

Per-Leg Asymmetric Gear Model NEW

2.0 treated the gear as a single unit following X-Plane's deploy ratio. Each main leg is now modelled independently, so one leg can lose hydraulic holding pressure and hang partially down and unlocked while the other stays up and locked. Reconciled against T.O. 1C-47-2 paragraphs 2-348, 2-356 and 2-409 and against Quebecair emergency procedures.

  • Hydraulic actuation in both directions, with an elastic bungee shock cord balancing gear weight during travel and a cable-operated three-position mechanical safety latch holding the actuating cylinder hook
  • Gravity extension is real: latch released, valve down, no pressure, and the leg falls under its own weight plus airload. Slowing down and yawing seats the safety latch, exactly as the operator procedure describes
  • Recovery is technique dependent. Hydraulic seating with the hand pump, or a low-speed slip, both work
  • Asymmetric drag, yaw and roll from a hanging leg are applied additively through the plugin force set, so X-Plane keeps doing all of its own gear and ground physics untouched
  • An unlatched leg collapses on the ground through the gear failure seam rather than a full override
  • Per-leg visual position is published for the 3D model to animate against
  • With no gear failure active the per-leg model tracks X-Plane's normal gear exactly, so taxi and normal operation are unchanged
  • The gear warning horn now triggers off per-leg down-and-locked state, not raw deploy ratio

Gear Handle Interlock and Lock Lever NEW

  • Three-tier interlock: safety clip, lock lever, then the gear control valve handle. Each must be dealt with in order
  • The lock lever physically interlocks the gear handle, and the LOCKED / UNLOCKED indicator lights track the lock lever rather than simply mirroring deploy state
  • Premature positive lock jam modelled, a real DC-3 failure mode where moving the lock lever too early jams the mechanism
  • The lock lever can be re-latched in flight
  • Stock X-Plane gear and flap key bindings are intercepted and consumed, so the G key runs the full DC-3 procedure instead of bypassing it, and refuses gear up on the ground
  • Dedicated gear lever popup window for users without a clear view of the pedestal

Flap System Behaviour ENHANCED

  • Spring-return flap lever with a drive-window latch, so the stock 1 and 2 keys give press-and-hold continuous flap motion rather than stepping to fixed presets
  • Flap travel is gated on hydraulic pressure and slows realistically in cold, thick fluid
  • Four flap failure modes wired to the hydraulic system: seal leak per side causing slow travel and creep under load, relief valve stuck open leaving the flaps inoperative, and asymmetric rigging producing a roll tendency on deployment
  • Gear and flap travel sounds are suppressed on aircraft reload so a reload no longer plays a phantom cycle

SURFACE DE-ICING BOOT SYSTEM

Pneumatic Boot Distributor NEW

In 2.0 the de-ice switch simply flipped X-Plane's own surface heat and boot switches. The C++ plugin models the actual installation per T.O. 1C-47-2 Section III paragraph 3-420 and Maintenance Manual Section C paragraphs 4-124 through 4-162.

  • Air supply from two engine-driven Type B-12 rotary four-vane positive-displacement pumps, one per engine, through oil separators and a pressure regulating separator
  • Regulated pressure 8 +/- 0.5 PSI, 10 PSI maximum
  • Electric distributor valve on the DEICER EQUIPMENT circuit breaker, rotating through five positions. One full revolution takes 40 seconds, each section inflating for roughly 8 seconds
  • Inflation sequence: outboard wing centre cells, outboard wing upper and lower cells, inboard wing centre cells, inboard wing upper and lower cells, then the horizontal and vertical stabiliser boots
  • Switching off does not stop the system dead. The distributor motor keeps running until the rotor reaches the exhaust port position, venting pump air overboard and deflating all boots
  • Feeds directly into the icing engine's shedding model, so inflation pressure and cycle position determine how much ice actually comes off

MODERN VARIANT AVIONICS ADDITIONS

KCS 55A Compass System, Full Component Model NEW

2.0 drew the KI 525A as a gauge reading X-Plane's heading directly. The compass system is now modelled as its four real line-replaceable units, referenced to King Installation Manual 006-00111-0010 Rev February 2002 and the KCS 55A Pilot's Guide.

  • KMT 112 magnetic azimuth transmitter (flux valve): wingtip-mounted flux gate sensing the earth's field. Models bank-induced dip error, where a banked wing tilts the valve out of the local horizontal plane and it picks up the vertical component of the field, with error scaling by bank angle and magnetic dip. Optional installer deviation correction. Signal is lost when the KG 102A is unpowered
  • KG 102A directional gyro: the core of the system, hosting the slaving circuitry and powering the rest of it. Slaving sensitivity +/- 0.5 degrees, slow slave rate 3 degrees per minute, fast slave 180 degrees per minute. Unlike the older KCS 55 it fast-slaves until the error is actually zero rather than stopping on a timer. Tumble detection triggers auto fast-slave above 25 degrees per second of yaw rate.   • Mirrored state refreshed for the gauges the client actually reads
